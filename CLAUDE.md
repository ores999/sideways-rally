# Sideways Rally

A browser rally game in a single HTML file. It has 4 stages, 7 cars that are parodies of real rally cars, a custom WebGL2 renderer, a physics model, and a co-driver that reads pace notes. There are no dependencies and no bundler: `build.py` concatenates the sources.

## Working with the owner
- The game is in **English** (UI, notes, car sheets). The owner speaks Spanish (Argentina). Reply in Spanish and use voseo.
- Cars use their **real names** (the owner asked for this; they used to be parodies like "Fjord Foca"): Ford Focus RS WRC, Subaru Impreza WRC, Mitsubishi Lancer Evo VI, Lancia Delta HF Integrale, Peugeot 205 T16, Audi Sport Quattro S1, Toyota Celica GT-Four. The make is lettered on the livery, `note` in `CARS` is the one-line blurb under the name in the garage, and the internal ids (`foca`, `impressa`, `lancha`, `delta`, `pejo`, `kwattro`, `selica`) stay as they are because saves and settings are keyed on them. Sponsors on the liveries are still made up.
- Effects should be noticeable without looking fake ("que se note pero que no se vea trucho"). Keep everything subtle.

## Layout
```
build.py          concatenates src/page.html + <script> blocks → dist/
src/page.html     markup + CSS (menu with Stage/Car/Options tabs, per-car Customise block in the Car tab, garage spec sheet, HUD, pause, results). Glass panels (`--glass`, `--sheen`), start-light countdown (`.lights`), metallic medals, entry animations
src/core.js       RallyCore: cars, stages, track and terrain generation, physics (stepCar/dynamics), AI, collisions
src/gl.js         GFX: mat4, geometry builder, shaders, renderer (shadow map, HDR+bloom post, particles, trails)
src/carmodel.js   CARMODEL: procedural low-poly car bodies (extruded profile with 3-facet rounded shoulders, `bevel`; perimeter faces, roof, windscreen, rear glass, sunstrip, bumpers (`rrect` with arc corners) and lamp lenses are split into columns so `warpCar` can curve them: one smooth field applied to body, glass and lamps adds a crown on top (0.05 m), bows the nose (0.055) and tail (0.07) in plan, draws the sides in towards both ends and adds tumblehome; normals are rebuilt per triangle afterwards, then `smoothNormals` (36° crease angle) averages them at shared positions so curves shade smoothly while creases stay sharp; the big side-panel triangles are in `g.lock` and stay flat, since smoothing them streaks across the doors. Wheels are smoothed at 30°. Wings are `airfoil` sections (cambered, angled) on pylons with thin endplates, the exhaust is a chrome `tube`, the lamps have a glossy `lens` cover mesh (drawn at alpha 0.18), and rubber seals run along the side windows; `fender` blisters on every arch, wider on `flares` shapes; `prism`/`rrect` bumpers with cut corners and wedge mirrors; roof chamfer; bonnet louvres), separate tinted `glass` mesh (drawn in the transparent pass, alpha 0.8) over an `interior` (tub, dashboard placed behind the windscreen slope, bucket seats, helmets, steering wheel, roll cage), crew-name stickers on the rear side glass (atlas extras row, `CREW`), light units (housings, twin lamps, segmented tails), livery canvas atlas with panel gaps and baked shading, wheels (rounded tyre shoulders, staggered tread, bulging sidewall, dished rim with thick spokes and centre nut, vented disc; `caliper` is its own mesh drawn with `wheelMatrix(i, true)` so it doesn't spin) + motion-blur discs (`wheelBlur`, drawn in the transparent pass with alpha from wheel speed; the drawn spin rate eases off past ~65 km/h so spokes don't strobe)
src/world.js      WORLD: stage palettes (VIS), colour grading, terrain/road meshes, trees, ground cover, props
src/game.js       game loop, UI, input, audio (per-car engine synthesis), co-driver, camera, suspension visuals, effects, HUD
dist/test.html    standalone page (with doctype). Open this to play locally
dist/sideways-rally.html  same without the doctype (the claude.ai artifact host adds it)
test/             node physics tests + python Playwright screenshot/flow tests
```
Build: `python3 build.py`. To play, open `dist/test.html` in Chrome.

## Rendering (gl.js)
- Instanced meshes, stride 11 per instance: `x,y,z,rot, sxz,sy,variant,0, r,g,b`. `a_i1.z` shifts the atlas u (ground cover variants).
- Lit shader modes: 0 vertex colour, 1 procedural road, 2 alpha-tested texture, 3 rock/props (broad noise, strata bands, speckle and hairline cracks keyed on world position), 4 emissive, 5 car livery (tex × vertex colour; plus metallic paint grain and sparse sun-catching flakes keyed on model-space `v_lp`, so they ride with the car), 6 decal (contact shadow), 7 ground cover (alpha-to-coverage, distance fade), 8 foliage (needle streaks along each face using a coordinate across the face `fu`, clumps and bright tips; bark furrows/knots on trunks (flagged by `v_uv.y < -0.5` and a dark colour); cactus ribs and spines (flagged by spec 0.02); a glow when the sun is behind; used by trees, cacti, shrubs and hay), 9 ground (terrain, verge strips, snow banks: broad variation + hue patches + two layers of a tiling detail texture, bump from its luminance).
- Ground detail textures (`groundDetailCanvas` in world.js, 512², linear RGBA8, repeat, mipmaps, aniso 8, made per stage and sampled at 1/3.4 m and 1/1.17 m rotated): pine = grass blades and clover dots, forest = needle litter, twigs, moss and pebbles, mesa = wind ripples (crest + shadow lines) and grain, frost = drifts, faint ripples, crust and glints. Neutral grey 128 means no change; the shader multiplies albedo by `d1*d2*4`. To add a stage, add a branch there. `R.texture(canvas, { repeat, linear })` takes the options. The verge strips take the colour of the terrain vertex nearest to their outer edge (`W.TC`), so they melt into the ground.
- Livery weathering is baked into the atlas (dust behind the wheels, tar/stone speckle along the sills, chips on the leading edges, scratches; per-car deterministic) on top of the dynamic `dirt` uniform. The reflection environment (`envRefl`) now includes the warm haze lobe around the sun.
- Far scenery (`farGeo`): Red Mesa's buttes have a talus skirt and 3–4 strata bands taken from `VIS.mesa.strata`. The other stages get procedural low-poly mountains (rings of jittered points under an off-centre peak, darker at the foot, paler rock higher up, snow on top) in two rows; the outer row is taller and hazier.
- Shadows: two cascades side by side in one 4096×2048 depth atlas (`beginShadow(0)` near, `beginShadow(1)` far; cascade 0 clears the atlas). In game.js, near = 32 m square about 7 m ahead of the car (~1.5 cm a texel), far = 140 m square 30 m ahead. The lit VS writes `v_sp`/`v_sp2` with a normal offset of ~1.2 texels per cascade (`env.shNo`), plus a small constant bias. `shadowF` blends near→far at the near cascade's edge, fades the far one out at its own edge, and filters with 12 rotated Poisson taps (`PD`, each a hardware 2×2 compare). Cost: ~0.25 ms at 2560×1600.
- `spec` (vertex colour alpha) drives gloss: Fresnel sky reflection (`envRefl`) + sun glint. `spec >= 0.75` = bare metal.
- HDR path: `R.beginScene(w,h,settings.post)` → MSAA RGBA16F → resolve → bloom chain (dual filter) → `R.endScene(E.grade)` composite with ACES, per-stage grade (lift/gain/sat/contrast/vignette in `VIS[stage].grade`). With no float buffers, or with the "Glow and colour grading" option off, the shaders tonemap directly (`u_hdr`).
- Tyre tracks use multiplicative blending (`DST_COLOR, ZERO`).
- Culling: `setLitFrame`/`beginShadow` load the pass's frustum planes. A mesh with `chunks` (`{first, count, box}`) draws only runs of visible chunks (`drawRuns`). The terrain builds its index buffer in 24×24-cell blocks (`terrainMesh`). Instanced props pass `{ cell, cullDist }` to `R.mesh`, which sorts instances by ground cell and re-points the instance attributes per run. Tufts are cut at `folFade[1]` and props past the fog. This took the main pass from ~2.8 M to ~0.6 M vertices and the shadow pass from 0.8 M to 0.09 M. Chunked instanced meshes must not be updated with `setInstances` (the fans aren't chunked).
- Performance: dynamic resolution in game.js (`dyn`, judged on 1.5 s windows, scale 0.6–1, frames over 1 s ignored). At minimum scale and still under 40 fps it drops the post chain for the session (`dyn.noPost`). On SwiftShader at 1280×720 (a stand-in for a very weak GPU), culling made frames 1.4–1.85× faster and dynamic resolution + post fallback took fps from 2.7 to 4.3. MSAA 2× when the effective DPR ≥ 1.5, else 4× (`beginScene` sample hint). Bloom chain in `R11F_G11F_B10F` when renderable.
- Particles: on clean tarmac (`SURF_FX.mesa[0]`) the road dust is only a faint haze (k 0.02, alpha 0.1). Tyre smoke while sliding is a separate path. Heavier settings showed as separate grey balls behind the car on straights.
- Headless screenshots: a scratch script launched Playwright with `channel='chrome'` and `--use-angle=metal` on the file:// build. That renders with the GPU, takes shots through `window.__rally` and leaves no windows open.
- Light shafts: the sky writes alpha 0 into the HDR target, so alpha marks sky vs geometry. `RAYS_FS` marches from each pixel towards the sun's screen position over that sky mask (only near the sun), at quarter resolution. The composite adds it × sun colour × `VIS[stage].rays` (forest 0.45, frost 0.45, pine 0.35, mesa 0.3), faded as the sun leaves the screen (game.js before `endScene`). It costs ~0.2 ms at 2048×1536 when the sun is in view. Don't make the sky output alpha 1 again.
- Clouds and their shadows share one field (`CLOUDS` chunk in gl.js: `cfbm`, `cbillow`, `cloudN`, `cloudUV`, `cloudLo`). The cumulus sit on a plane 900 m up, fixed in the world and drifting with the wind. The plane height doesn't change how the sky looks: it only sets their size on the ground (a few hundred metres) and the parallax as you drive. The sky samples it from the camera; the lit VS samples it from each vertex toward the sun (`v_cs`, per vertex because the field is hundreds of metres wide), so the shadow on the ground is the shadow of the cloud you see, and the sun is hidden when you stand in one. Over a run about 30% of Pine Ridge, 39% of Frostvik and 13% of Dark Pines is under cloud; Red Mesa's sparse sky leaves it clear.
- Lighting extras: fog with sun in-scatter (`u_glw`, the same term as the sky's horizon so there is no seam), and shade baked into terrain vertex colours under trees and rocks. Pines get a vertex-colour gradient (dark base, light tips).

## Physics (core.js)
- Two-axle bicycle model, Pacejka-lite `sin(1.45·atan(x))`, friction circle, weight transfer, downforce, turbo/boost model, gearbox from ratios + final drive, AWD split, ABS-like brake limit when steering.
- Assists, all in `TUNE`: turn-in assist, slide "hold" (yaw target), **trail braking** (`trail`, `trailK`: braking while turning brings yaw up to a grip-limited target), `lowGrip` (extra bite under ~70 km/h for hairpins), `lockK`, per-axle surface grip blend at the road edge (`edgeA/edgeB/edgeMix`).
- Slide traction (`slideA/slideB` = 0.03/0.2 rad of axle slip angle): an axle that is already sliding keeps the same lateral share as a turned wheel (`tcF/tcR`), so flooring it out of a slide pulls the car straight instead of spinning both axles up. Before this, a handbrake slide + straight wheel + full throttle took 1.1–1.3 s to grip again; now ~0.5 s. Starting the reserve at small angles (0.03) also stops the pendulum after a countersteer (Peugeot/Audi went from ~0.85 s to ~0.35 s). Normal driving, power-on oversteer and AI times are unchanged. `slideF` scales the front reserve (1 measured best).
- Spin catch (`spinK/spinB/spinV` = 5 / 0.5 rad / 12 m/s): past ~29° of slip and still rotating further into it, part of the yaw rate is bled off. It never acts with the handbrake held and only fades in above ~43 km/h, so handbrake hairpins keep their times. Measured with `human.js` over 12 aggression levels (216 runs), compared with the handling before these changes: spins 56→13, resets 32→18, hits 61→41, off-road 448→369 s, and slightly faster overall.
- Keyboard steering rate depends on speed (fast at hairpin speed, gentler at high speed).
- Stages: `STAGES` in core.js; each has an `id` (saves, `VIS` palette) and a `kind` (pine/frost/mesa) that drives behaviour shared between stages: terrain shaping, tufts, dust, skid marks, surface sounds, camera roughness. **Dark Pines** (`id: 'forest'`, `kind: 'pine'`) is a narrower gravel road (hw 4.0) with rows of pines along both verges for the whole stage (`placeObjects`, ~6500 trees), a needle-litter floor, a low morning sun and a green haze (`VIS.forest`, with `tuftPer`/`tuftTint` overrides).
- Impacts (`collide`): bounce with restitution, and a speed loss that scales with how square the hit is (`TUNE.impactP`; 0 = the old flat 18% on every touch). A graze along a row of trunks costs a few percent, a head-on hit still stops the car. A full rigid-body impulse (contact point, friction, inertia) was tried: it wedged the car against trunks more often (keyboard-bot resets 23→44), so it was dropped. Measured on 4 stages × 12 aggressions against the old model: resets 23→19, spins 16→14, off-road 444→408 s.
- Car specs in `CARS`. Adding a car takes a `CARS` entry (core.js), a shape in `SHAPES` and a livery `case` in carmodel.js (side + top lanes), plus `ENGINES` (worklet voice) and `VOICES` (fallback synth) entries in game.js. The Celica (`selica`, #6, `liftback` shape) was added this way; check it with sim.js and the handling scripts, which pick up every car automatically. The Peugeot (`pejo`) was tamed (370 hp, smoother boost, frontGrip/rearGrip) because the owner found it undriveable.

## Tests (run from test/)
- `node sim.js "" 0.9`: AI rival over every stage and car. Expect 0 hits/resets for all (rival times come from this).
- `node hmetrics.js`, `node sweep.js`, `node brakes.js`, `node power.js`: handling metrics (turn-in, lift-off, brake-turn yaw, hairpin times, power-on slip).
- `node impact.js`: drive at a lone tree (head-on, offset, corner clip) and along a row of trees at 6°: speed kept, heading change, peak yaw.
- `node recover.js`: drift recovery (handbrake/lift-off slide, then full throttle straight / countersteer / keep turning): time until it grips again, peak slip, `~` pendulum, `!` spin. Pass `'{"slideA":5,"slideB":6}'` to see the old behaviour.
- `node human.js` / `node digital.js`: imperfect keyboard-style driver bots.
- `python3 flow.py`: full UI flow in headless Chromium (needs `pip install playwright && playwright install chromium`).
- `python3 gfx.py [--ab]`: screenshots per stage (chase + close-ups). `--ab` compares post FX on/off.
- The scripts pass SwiftShader flags (`--use-angle=swiftshader`) for headless servers. Drop them on a Mac to use the GPU.
- Debug hooks exist only on `file://`: `window.__rally.advance(sec, keys)` (e.g. `{ KeyW: true }` holds the throttle), `auto(true)` (AI drives), `view(p,l,fov)` (camera relative to the car), `pickCar(i)`, `startRace()`, `toMenu()`, `setDirt(v)`, `audio()` (→ `{ A, sfx, getTune }`; swap `A.ctx/A.master/A.noise` for an `OfflineAudioContext` to render one sound), `dbg`.

## Audio (game.js)
- Engine: `AUDIO_WORKLET` (registered from a Blob URL, data URL as fallback) holds two processors. `engine` is a combustion model: per-cylinder exhaust pulses shaped in crank angle (bulk + sharp blowdown front + `rasp` noise), cycle-to-cycle jitter (more on overrun), two open-pipe sections as damped feedback combs, silencer lowpass, airbox resonance + intake roar, valvetrain clatter, soft clip. Per-car character in `ENGINES` (`off`/`amp` per cylinder give the boxer burble and the five-cylinder warble). `crunch` makes stone clicks for loose surfaces.
- If the worklet can't load, the old oscillator synth (`VOICES`) is used. Turbo whistle + air rush, gear whine, tyre squeal (tonal, tarmac only) and pops/flutter run in the main graph.
- **Customisation (Car tab, sound only):** `settings.tune[carId] = { dump: 'bov'|'flutter', exhaust: 'stock'|'sport' }`, read through `getTune(id)` (default dump: flutter for cars with `VOICES[id].flutter > 0.5`, i.e. Lancia/Peugeot, else BOV). Clicking an option plays a preview on the car's own engine: `A.rev` runs a scripted blip (idle → 88 % of redline for 0.7 s → lift, `REV_T` 2.4 s) through the normal `updateAudio` path, so the dump and the exhaust crackle sound exactly as in a race. Sport exhaust overrides the `ENGINES` entry in `buildVoice` (muf ×1.22, rasp +0.1, sharp +0.06, drive +0.3, body peak −8 % freq / +2.5 dB, gain ×1.15) and crackles much more on the overrun; `A.voice.ex` makes `initAudio` rebuild the voice when it changes. Physics never reads it.
- Dump valve logic: a lift is the throttle closing from >0.5 to <0.1 within ~0.3 s (`A.thrPk`). It only vents if there is pressure to let go: `A.press` follows the boost while the throttle is held (rate 2.5/s) and bleeds off when it isn't, the throttle must have been held > 0.3 s (`held`, read before this frame resets it) and the last dump must be 0.5 s ago. So tapping W never vents; holding and lifting always does. H-pattern upshifts spend 55 % of the pressure.
- `sfx.bov`: a soft puff (650 Hz) and a broad hiss (highpass 1.7→1.1 kHz, +4 dB at 3.6 kHz), 30 ms attack, 0.3–0.7 s tail. No click and no resonant sweep: the first version had both and sounded like a whip. `sfx.flutter(amt, boost)`: compressor surge heard at the turbo inlet, built as air, not knocks: one noise source (highpass 260 Hz → lowpass whose cutoff opens 1.3→3.3 kHz with each puff → +5 dB at 1.15 kHz, plus a 520 Hz lowpassed body) under one envelope curve (`setValueCurveAtTime`, 1.5 kHz). The envelope is a soft swell × rounded pulses (`sin²(π·√phase)`, so no edges or clicks) over a floor of ~26 % (the air never stops), starting at 19–25 Hz and slowing to ~9 Hz as the wheel spins down, with ±8 % jitter per cycle; the whistle winds down to half pitch underneath, only lightly pulsed. The first version (hard 4 ms attacks, silent gaps, a resonant formant sweep per pulse) sounded mechanical, like something breaking. Offline levels per 20 ms: BOV ~0.14 peak (the owner likes it as it is), flutter ~0.11 peak, against ~0.05 of engine on the overrun in game.
- Exhaust bangs live in the engine worklet: a `pop` k-rate parameter (overrun crackle rate: 0.04 base, 0.1 with anti-lag, 0.3 sport, plus 0.6 sport / 0.25×antilag for ~1 s after a lift) turns some firings into small afterfires and some into misfires; `port.postMessage({ bang: size })` queues a big one that goes out with the next firing (anti-lag, limiter, shifts). A bang is a hard front plus a noisy tail through the same pipe combs, and part of it bypasses the silencer (`bl`). `sfx.pop` remains only for the oscillator fallback. Offline: crackle windows reach ~4× the overrun level. Harness: run the worklet in node with a stub processor that has `port = {}` and call `port.onmessage({ data: { bang } })`.

- Last published build: claude.ai artifact "Sideways Rally" (version with trail braking, hairpins and the Peugeot fix). The graphics pass (post FX, car reflections, ground cover) and the handling pass (edge grip, key steering) are done in `src/`.

- **Sky pass done** (`SKY_FS` in gl.js): scattering gradient (warm haze toward the sun, cooler away, horizon stays on the fog colour), a paler haze band just above the horizon, cirrus layer, an altocumulus sheet of small puffs in patches, cumulus with cauliflower rims (`billow` detail eating into the edge), darker bases when seen overhead, self-shadowing (density sampled toward the sun) and silver lining, soft sun disc + corona dimmed by cloud, stars at Frostvik (`stars` in `VIS`), dither against banding. The sky is drawn after opaque geometry in game.js so hidden pixels skip the shader.
- Tree LOD: an instanced mesh can carry `lod = { m, dist, shadowDist }` (a simpler mesh built from the same instance data, so the sorted chunks line up). `drawRuns` draws cells beyond `dist` from the camera with `m.lod.m` in the main pass, and beyond `shadowDist` in the shadow pass. Pines use `pineFarGeo` (bare 5-sided cones, ~27% of the vertices) past 80 m (30 m for shadows). Main pass: forest 426k→292k, frost 428k→329k. Shadow pass: forest 221k→121k, frost 137k→72k.
- Terrain LOD: `terrainMesh` builds `chunk.lods` (stride 1/2/4; borders keep every vertex and a zipper strip joins them to the coarse interior, so there are no cracks). Levels are laid out one after another in the index buffer. `drawRuns` picks the level per visible block by ground distance (`chunks.lodDist`, pushed out with the stage fog) in the main pass, and level 0 in the shadow pass. Terrain went from ~0.8 M to ~0.17 M indices per frame. Total main pass per stage: Pine 0.40 M, Frost 0.43 M, Mesa 0.28 M vertices (2.8 M before culling).
- Headless tests: the python tests need Playwright. A venv with `pip install playwright` plus `launch(channel='chrome')` uses the installed Chrome (no Chromium download). `flow.py` passed this way.
- Measuring GPU cost: timer queries return nothing on this Mac. Use throughput instead: N × `__rally.advance(0.05)` with one `readPixels` at the end.
- Debug-hook gotcha: `window.__rally` only exists on `file:`, and the in-app browser opens files as static snapshots. To test there, serve a copy of `dist/test.html` with the `location.protocol === 'file:'` check replaced by `true`.
